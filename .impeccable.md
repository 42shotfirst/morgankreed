## Design Context

### Users
A mixed audience of startup founders (Series A-C), VP/C-suite at mid-market companies, and PE/VC portfolio ops teams. They arrive evaluating whether Morgan is the right fractional technology leader for their specific situation. Mindset ranges from fast-moving founders who decide quickly to deliberate enterprise buyers doing due diligence. All share one thing: they need to trust this person with consequential technology decisions.

### Brand Personality
**Confident, direct, operator-grade.**

This is a senior technologist who has done this before — many times. The site should communicate earned authority without self-promotion. No fluff, no buzzwords, no hand-waving. Every element should feel like it was placed with the same precision Morgan brings to a client engagement. The voice is first-person plural when talking about work ("we ship, we fix, we lead") and plain-spoken, never salesy.

Three words: **decisive, credible, sharp.**

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark — refined navy foundation. The dark palette communicates seriousness and technical depth without being "developer dark mode."
- **Palette**: Keep the navy (#16162a) + cyan (#0ea5e9) identity. Refine rather than replace — tighten the supporting neutrals, ensure the cyan is used sparingly for maximum impact (accent, not wallpaper).
- **Typography**: Move beyond Inter. The site needs a typeface with more character — something that reads as intentional and curated, not default.
- **Motion**: Purposeful and restrained. Animations should feel like confidence (smooth, unhurried deceleration), not like showing off.
- **Terminal motif**: The `▮ cto-on-demand.inc` wordmark and command-line references are distinctive — keep them, but use sparingly as a texture, not a theme.

### Anti-References
- **NOT** a generic SaaS landing page (gradient blobs, stock photos, "trusted by 10,000+" social proof bars)
- **NOT** a developer portfolio (GitHub-green, project cards, "I build things" personal site energy)
- **NOT** a corporate consultancy (McKinsey blue, stock boardroom imagery, buzzword density)
- The site should feel like neither a product nor a resume — it's a firm.

### Design Principles
1. **Earned authority over claimed authority.** Show, don't tell. Metrics, outcomes, and specificity beat adjectives.
2. **Restraint is confidence.** Fewer elements, more impact. Whitespace is a statement. Cyan is powerful because it's rare on the page.
3. **Precision in every detail.** Typography, spacing, alignment, and motion should all feel deliberate — as if a senior designer made every choice on purpose.
4. **Substance over spectacle.** No animation without purpose. No decoration without function. Every element earns its place.
5. **Firm, not freelancer.** The visual language should communicate an established practice, not a solo contractor looking for gigs.
